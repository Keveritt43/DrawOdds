## Product baseline

- Use the existing Idaho deer and elk experience as the default reference when building or changing another state or species. Compare the actual Idaho pages and behavior: navigation, formatting, responsive layout, hunt cards, graphs and their sizing, maps and shading, filters, hunt and unit details, scoring explanations, and available data elements. A hunter familiar with Idaho should find a consistent experience elsewhere.
- Adapt the baseline where a state's regulations, draw system, species, source reports, or available data differ. Preserve useful state-specific features such as Colorado points, draw odds by points, and point creep. Use the appropriate agency source and species-specific information; do not copy Idaho values or imply an unavailable metric exists. Clearly label missing, estimated, or non-comparable data and its source or time period where relevant.
- Keep species distinct where appropriate while allowing users to search the same species across supported states. Group comparable filters across states; label state-specific filters and apply them only to their applicable states. Preserve selected filters when navigating between results and hunt details.
- Scores and comparisons should use validated, explainable inputs and be calibrated for the relevant species and state; a given harvest rate may mean different things for different species. Do not treat missing inputs as zero or claim estimates are exact. For overlapping seasons, require a shared unit and actual overlapping open dates, avoid double counting, and identify any hunter-pressure proxy. For deer hunts, consider concurrent general/OTC elk pressure in elbow room and quality where supported by the data.
- When adding a state or species, carry the work through the relevant data sources, parsing and validation, search, cards, details, maps, charts, filters, and score explanations as applicable. Check representative examples and responsive layouts against Idaho, run appropriate automated checks and user-journey UAT, and fix relevant issues found. Clearly report any remaining gaps rather than presenting partial support as complete.

## Draw odds and metric integrity

- For future western-state source leads and draw-system research cautions, consult `docs/western-state-research.md`; reverify current agency sources and rules before implementation.
- Implement and test a draw-odds adapter for each state and draw system using its current official rules and published reports. Account for applicable residency, species, points and point type, license/choice, applicant pool, quota and eligibility; do not assume that applicants, applications, licenses and tags are interchangeable or that all points systems work like Colorado's. Document the formula, inputs, source, reporting years, pool and assumptions. Use official historical results when available and label model-based estimates or projections separately; a past result or projection is not a guarantee.
- Only calculate a rate when its numerator and denominator describe the same population, geography, season, choice and period. Preserve source granularity and distinguish a pooled multi-year rate from an average of annual rates. Do not infer exact point-level odds or availability from a cutoff, an OTC classification, or incomplete reports. A zero denominator, missing pool, suppressed count or unsupported case is unknown, not 0% or 100%; show an appropriate explanation.
- Define each displayed metric consistently across cards, charts, filters, maps, comparisons and exports, and expose the source, years, units and aggregation when needed to interpret it. Harvest success is harvested animals divided by participating hunters for matching reported data; hunter density uses an explicitly identified hunter count and area/period. Public land is not automatically accessible or huntable. Point creep describes a measured change in observed point cutoffs, not guaranteed future points. Where draw-and-fill is shown as draw odds × harvest success, label it as an estimate with the assumptions and compatible time/population basis, not a promise of an animal.
- When comparing across states or species, distinguish genuinely comparable metrics from agency-specific definitions, differing years, sample sizes and geographic coverage. Keep raw observations separate from derived scores and user-specific calculations; preserve unknowns and rounding consistency. Validate against representative official report examples, including edge cases and point/residency pools, and test that the same inputs yield consistent results across the user journey.
