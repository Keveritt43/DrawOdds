# Western harvest and trophy audit — November 15, 2026

## Scope and comparison method

The owner-supplied `western_top_tier_units-v5.csv` was used only as a reference; its SHA-256 is `8a39464a3c56ad63efd62ffdeb7ea5e959fd968e67763d3035f6c92d6df25860`. It contains 2,433 records for 11 states, including states not supported by DrawOdds. It has the same record IDs as the v4 source already in the repository; added species/agency aliases and explicit `Unranked` labels do not by themselves establish new trophy evidence. The supplied numerical trophy scores are not the same calculation as DrawOdds' discrete trophy scores or its Quality index.

A read-only public export of the combined finder, separately requested for deer, elk and pronghorn on November 15, yielded 3,953 hunt rows with default settings and eligibility filters. It is not a complete inventory of restricted, youth, private-land-only or other omitted hunts. For each scored hunt and each unit, the audit used the existing species-scoped source resolver (including Utah's geographic crosswalk and Wyoming prefixes) to find the reference record. It compared the reference tier with the live **whole-hunt** trophy score; counts below are hunt-unit comparisons, not independent units or confirmed errors. Multi-unit floor rules, dates, legal animals, source uncertainty and unmatched boundaries can explain differences. Duplicate reference subspecies with conflicting labels were left ambiguous.

## Production scoring checks

A read-only production snapshot of all 1,303 Idaho summaries was compared with `scripts/recalculate-idaho-scores.ts` inputs, including private-land handling, season restrictions and the current `scoreHunts` model. All seven stored score columns matched for all 1,303 rows. This check did not write scores. Colorado recalculates through its Colorado adapter; Montana, Utah and Washington recalculate on read through `withStateTrophyScores`. Wyoming deer success and dependent scores are intentionally suppressed because its species hunter counts overlap by an unknown amount; Wyoming elk and antelope license-type rates are also withheld from unique-hunter metrics and dependent scores because WGFD reports active licenses; trophy tiers remain available. Live public exports verify the displayed scoring path for the exported rows; they do not independently validate every input, restricted hunt, stored non-Idaho score, or Colorado overlap context.

The legacy Idaho export used uncorrected stored fields for hunt 1044, including Unit 57 and geometry-derived land and density. The shared finder and details correctly use the verified Unit 55 only and suppress unsupported land/density/Quality. This change applies the correction and current trophy score to the legacy export and its affected filters.

## Harvest identity audit and correction

* Idaho controlled harvest is linked through historical draw lineage and area rather than the current number alone; individual area/boundary lineage remains dependent on its source data. No Idaho agency synchronization was performed.
* Colorado uses agency hunt-code or unit/season harvest depending on source; unit-level data are not necessarily hunt-code observations. The existing documentation records coverage limitations; this audit did not independently revalidate all seasons and boundaries.
* Montana combines agency district harvest with surveyed years and historical boundary checks; it does not use Washington's choice-number join. District-level estimates do not establish permit-specific results.
* Wyoming uses agency area/type reports; deer combined hunter overlap remains unknown and is suppressed as described above. Renumbered or changed areas still require boundary-specific investigation.
* Utah joins limited-entry harvest by hunt code. Among 2021–2025 source reports, 129 codes have different name strings; that is a review queue, not 129 proven errors. Examples include shortened names such as `South Slope, Diamond Mtn` / `Diamond Mtn` and `5S` / `5S Land and Livestock`. A complete season, boundary and code-reuse audit remains open.
* Washington has a confirmed general bug: its original historical harvest join used the present choice number and name even when choice numbers moved. The individually reviewed Desert 1079 and 1091 histories were corrected earlier. This audit confirms 2026 late-archery hunt 1080 corresponds to hunt 1081 in 2021–2025, rather than the early-archery hunt 1080. WDFW reports 12 harvests in 44 hunter-seasons, or 27.3% pooled; the prior 30.3% was the early hunt's 10/33. The annual pamphlets, tag, area, legal animal and season support this match. Quality and trophy must be recomputed using the corrected history. A broader candidate rebuild changes hundreds of Washington histories, including some removals; it has not been reviewed or applied. Further state-wide identity work remains necessary.

Source material: [WDFW annual game harvest reports](https://wdfw.wa.gov/hunting/management/game-harvest), the repository's Washington annual pamphlets and harvest report snapshots, Utah DWR reports and state onboarding documentation, and the current application score and state adapter implementations. Missing or ambiguous evidence remains unknown, not zero. Production state-data writes require a matching reviewed dry-run digest and counts, and verification that other states are unchanged.

## Follow-up: Washington identity audit

The earlier candidate was not applied at the time of the comparison above. The subsequent follow-up uses the stricter annual identity and season-date checks documented in `states/wa-onboarding.md`, archives the prior attributed histories, and keeps genuinely ambiguous years out of displayed rates. It examined 766 existing special-permit rows in a fresh production snapshot: the reviewed plan changes 687, including 299 gaining comparable history and 42 losing a previously attributed rate. ## Follow-up: Utah and Wyoming boundary evidence

DWR's public 2025 Utah boundary layer contained 152 polygons. Comparing each named BoundaryID with the retained 2026 geometry using Turf area intersection yielded 151 with at least 98% intersection-over-union; the remaining boundary 904 was 86.8%, and no current bundled hunt uses boundary 904. The three polygon errors from simplification were rechecked with geometry repair and had IoU above 99%. Of 617 bundled Utah hunts, 421 use only boundaries in the >=98% group and 419 of those have a 2025 harvest observation. This checks geographic overlap, not proof that every historic hunt code belonged to the same 2025 boundary. The DWR 2024 service returned `Token Required`; 2021–2024 hunt-to-boundary joins remain unverified. Source: DWR `Utah_Big_Game_Hunt_Boundaries_2025` ArcGIS FeatureServer, layer 0, retrieved for this audit.

WGFD documents the 2024 combination of elk areas 53/54 and movement of part of 54 into 65, and the 2025 boundary changes to 117, 123 and 126. The application now uses only post-change observations for those areas and for region aggregates containing them, retaining the original raw observations. These filters are applied to hunt details, finder, export, unit views and maps before dependent scoring. This is a conservative year-based rule, not a full historical Wyoming polygon audit.

## Wyoming active-license denominator

WGFD's [2025 elk harvest report](https://wgfd.wyo.gov/media/33482/download) distinguishes active licenses in license-type rows from unique hunters in pooled rows. The per-license reports are not established unique-hunter counts, and region totals may repeat holders across areas. Wyoming elk and antelope now retain the labeled harvest-per-active-license data but withhold hunter success, density, draw-and-fill, Quality and Opportunity until a compatible denominator is established. This is conservative even for license types whose one-license rules may allow future validation; it does not delete historical observations or change trophy tiers. The published boundary corrections still filter the underlying license observations.

## Follow-up: 2019–2025 source audit

A read-only Colorado production audit compared 2,991 unit-based hunt-year observations in multi-unit hunts with their matching stored 2019–2024 CPW species/season tables. It found 331 incomplete observations on 107 hunts. The read-time correction retains the raw series and excludes only exact matched observations; it recalculates pooled success, density and dependent scores. The next import now rejects partial unit coverage. This does not prove unit boundaries or seasons were unchanged in every other observation.

For Montana, 15 mule deer permit hunts used estimated mule bucks divided by permits issued, and 10 general hunts used one deer species' harvest divided by all deer hunters. Neither establishes matching hunter success; those rates remain labeled district context, and no longer drive Quality or Opportunity. The district hunting-pressure estimate is retained separately. The audit did not independently revalidate every Montana hunt's season and district boundaries.

Wyoming's previous nonresident general elk regions included limited-quota hunt areas inside geographic region polygons. WGFD's 2025 and 2026 Chapter 7 section 6 list the same restricted sets of eligible areas. WGFD's 2025 survey table 5 provides direct unique region-license results (E 719/962, S 511/1083, W 1506/2772), avoiding repeated license holders in area sums. Corrected region geography and the single-year rates are applied on read; historical data remain stored. No land or density is inferred from the former larger polygons.

For Utah, the agency 2021 application guide identifies DB1330 as Iron Spring, while its 2021 harvest report calls the same code Indian Spring; the 2022 guide and later reports say Iron Spring. The 2025 application guide explicitly calls Circle E Ranch formerly J.B. Ranch (DB1253); EB3529 refers to the same renamed ranch. Five 2025 changed general deer units have new hunt codes, and their current histories begin in 2025. These identity checks do not establish every historical polygon or rule out other boundary changes. The 2021–2024 DWR boundary geometry remains unavailable without authorization to the agency's service. Sources: https://wildlife.utah.gov/guidebooks/2021_biggameapp.pdf, https://wildlife.utah.gov/pdf/bg/2021/2021_le_oial_all.pdf, https://wildlife.utah.gov/guidebooks/2022_biggameapp.pdf, https://wildlife.utah.gov/guidebooks/biggameapp.pdf, and https://wildlife.utah.gov/news/2024/12/13/utah-wildlife-board-approves-updated-deer-management-plan-new-hunt-strategies-research-and-other-items.

Colorado follow-up: 70 male- or female-only deer and elk hunts also had 277 observations in 2021–2024 that divided a sex-specific harvest by all hunters in a unit table. These are not matching populations. The application now omits those all-sex-denominator observations for sex-specific licenses, including older retained years, and future imports require a sex-specific split or hunt-code report. Either-sex licenses may still use the all-sex unit tables. Source: CPW harvest table definitions and `src/sync/co/harvest.ts`; raw observations remain stored.

## 2024 Utah polygon and Colorado boundary follow-up

DWR publishes a public 2024 **antlerless** hunt-boundary layer, separate from its token-protected 2024 big-game service. Of its 94 boundary IDs, 93 were compared to current bundled polygons and all had at least 99% intersection over union after simplification. Boundary 240 could not be reliably compared because of geometry errors and is not used by a bundled hunt. Of 617 bundled Utah hunts, 191 use only the 93 compared IDs; 180 of those have a 2024 harvest observation. This verifies similarity of those polygons, not that every 2024 buck/bull hunt was assigned to the same boundary, or any 2021–2023 polygon. Evidence and method: `data/ut/boundary-2024-audit.json`; source https://services.arcgis.com/ZzrwjTRez6FJiOq4/arcgis/rest/services/Utah_Antlerless_Hunts_2024/FeatureServer/0.

Colorado CPW split unit 43 and moved land from 79 to 791 for 2024; it realigned the shared 97/99 boundary near Brush for 2026. Read-time harvest corrections and future summary builds now exclude pre-change observations when a hunt uses only one side, or has a partial-unit restriction. Full unions of both changed units are retained; unit-level pressure still requires a compatible boundary for each unit. CPW describes the 97/99 adjustment as a short realignment near Brush, but does not report harvest for the transferred area separately. Original reports and summaries are preserved. See CPW commission documents https://cpw.state.co.us/sites/default/files/dam/dhzf8lq2am/item.10-w-0_final.pdf and https://cpw.state.co.us/sites/default/files/dam/pqrze0mlax/item.27.3_ch-w2_issues_consent.pdf.

Montana follow-up: FWP hunters can appear in more than one district or portion. The existing data builder sums district counts for multi-district hunts and some split district estimates; those sums are district participations, not verified unique hunters. The app now labels the ratio as harvest per participation and withholds hunter success, unique-hunter density and dependent scores for these series. In the bundled snapshot this covers 57 hunts, 49 with harvest history. The agency historical-district raster crosswalk remains a 95% approximate-overlap screen, not a guarantee that individual permit boundaries or season definitions stayed unchanged. See the source methodology in `scripts/mt_build_harvest.ts` and `data/mt/district-history.json`.

## Further Utah 2024 and season identity audit

A third-party-hosted ArcGIS copy labeled `BigGame_HuntBoundaries_2024` credits UDWR. We did not assume its provenance: 93 matching boundary IDs independently compared to DWR’s official 2024 antlerless layer all exceeded 99.5% intersection over union (one comparison could not be repaired reliably). Against current bundled polygons, 193 unique IDs exceeded 98%, including four repaired geometry comparisons; one geometry remains unresolved and two rows have placeholder ID 0. Among 617 bundled hunts, 394 use only those matched IDs and 372 have a 2024 harvest observation. For 341, the 2024 agency harvest row also has an exact normalized boundary name and hunt code. These checks strengthen the evidence but do not prove every 2024 assignment or any 2021–2023 boundary. See `data/ut/big-game-copy-2024-audit.json`, https://services3.arcgis.com/jZsUvKlJVFEJWsCV/ArcGIS/rest/services/Logan_s_Final_Project_WFL1/FeatureServer/4, and DWR official 2024 antlerless source in the previous section.

The 2026 hunt source identified 22 single-season September, late or restricted archery hunts that were incorrectly classified as any weapon; one multiseason hunt also had its restricted archery segment misclassified. Nine HAMSS hunts were shown as any weapon despite equipment restrictions, and restricted rifle/muzzleloader labels were lost. The 2026 DWR guide confirms the September and late archery hunts: https://wildlife.utah.gov/pdf/guidebooks/biggameapp.pdf. The display, date-and-method filters, season breakdown and read-time scoring now use the verified agency methods; HAMSS matches archery and muzzleloader filters without claiming unrestricted weapons. Separately, the agency marks 195 current hunt date ranges as uncertain (mostly CWMU operator-set windows). These are now labeled as windows, excluded from exact-date matches, and not used as evidence for trophy, Quality or Opportunity ratings. The correction is applied only to exact matching 2026 code and date windows; source history is retained.
