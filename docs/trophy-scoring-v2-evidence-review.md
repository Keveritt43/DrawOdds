# Trophy scoring v2: evidence and implementation review

The owner requested the v2 architecture in the attached guide. This review records what must be established before the model can replace the production score. It is not a scoring specification or a production rollout plan.

## Available inputs

The owner's `western_top_tier_units-v5.csv` contains 2,433 records (SHA-256 `8a39464a3c56ad63efd62ffdeb7ea5e959fd968e67763d3035f6c92d6df25860`). Its `trophy_tier`, `trophy_score`, `evidence_confidence`, `hunt_scope`, `source_tags`, and `active_status` values match the corresponding v4 CSV by record ID; v5 adds fields and changes some underlying tier entries and normalized IDs. Supplied publication names and county associations are evidence leads, not independently verified genetic measurements. A county record is not a unit-specific animal or a count of animals available to a hunt. Source lists cannot establish publication independence on their own.

The owner supplied a 264-row crosswalk, retained byte-for-byte as `data/ut/trophy-hunt-unit-crosswalk.csv` (SHA-256 `9037caab9b62ba61457c42f6da16b5932a65345070515a82c419faf01e4459dd`). It contains nine exact-code links and 255 name-substring links. The existing `data/trophy-ut-wmu-crosswalk.json` contains 292 species-scoped geographic candidate links, requiring at least 90% containment in a single management unit. Comparing the 617 retained current hunts found 318 agreements, 213 without a supplied match, 45 with incomplete geometry, 39 with ambiguous name matches and two conflicts. `scripts/audit-ut-hunt-unit-crosswalk.py` records each case and source digests. Agreement is geographic context, not proof of identical boundaries, record locations or historical identities. The two conflicts are Old Woman Plateau CWMU deer DB1272 and elk EB3540: the name match says management unit 25 while the geographic association says 16. These are withheld for review rather than silently resolved.

## Specific checks

- **Washington:** The 2026 WDFW legal descriptions confirm Deer Areas 1008 and 1009 are portions of GMU 169, 1031 is within 117, 1035 is within 121, and 2010 is within 284. These are subareas, not whole-GMU hunts. They are now available as explicitly labeled context in the offline v5 comparison; their 2026 descriptions do not prove older boundaries. Source: https://wdfw.wa.gov/sites/default/files/publications/02706/wdfw02706.pdf, pp. 95–96.
- **Wyoming:** Type 0 is specialty weapon excluding archery, not necessarily muzzleloader. Single-full-price-license rules help establish whether a license-type success ratio can describe a hunter population, but do not prove total concurrent hunter density or historical boundary compatibility. Sources: https://wgfd.wyo.gov/media/28637/download and https://wgfd.wyo.gov/media/33375/download.
- **Utah:** Hunt-code prefix is not a reliable license category: existing 2026 `DB13xx` examples include CWMU hunts, so a prefix must not be treated as Dedicated Hunter evidence. The v5 Zion deer scope explicitly includes general and dedicated-hunter seasons. Use the agency hunt table, eligibility, dates and actual boundary.
- **Timing:** A local `BC_mule_deer_all_records.csv` (SHA-256 `d73b031ebc469adbb2f402467d4d4342bf21b03c738ac6a105fdef7c259eec5f`) contains 4,654 records, 2,049 with parseable dates from 2000 onward. Using those 2,049 as the denominator gives 345 October 21–31, 421 November 1–10 and 251 November 11–20 records; this simple filter does not reproduce the guide's stated sample or percentages. Another 120 modern records have January 1 as the date, which requires verification before interpreting exact dates. The file's extraction provenance, selection filters and hunt-effort denominators are not documented; corresponding elk and pronghorn data were not found in Downloads. The proposed fixed timing curves and statewide application cannot yet be validated as hunt success or mature-animal probability. A midpoint also loses separate seasons, dates near both sides of a rut, and variation across states or populations. Missing and operator-set dates must remain unknown rather than receiving a numerical `6.0` labeled unscored.
- **Formula:** The owner clarified that there should be no private-land score deduction and no weapon offset. Low or incompletely mapped land access should be a warning rather than an additional trophy adjustment. The guide still does not define aggregation of multiple seasons/units and point populations, how unmeasured agency antler/age data maps to 40 points, or how state/species-specific thresholds and exceptions are validated. Its text calls trophy potential a genetic ceiling; the inputs do not establish a genetic ceiling. A claim of `Verified` must not depend only on two named sources without evidence they are independent and accurately apply to the hunt.
- **Ingestion:** Similar names, dates and 95% polygon overlap can suggest a lineage but do not prove same hunt, eligible population or season. Any automatic link needs an evidence-backed identity, historical boundaries, year-specific regulations, and a review queue; unknown geometry is not a passed overlap check.

## Release gates

Do not publish a new numerical model from incomplete inputs or invent biology, hunt identity or confidence. Establish source provenance, review the supplied crosswalk conflicts, define unknown cases, and prepare reproducible hunt-level shadow results for each residency and state. Review distributions, boundary and eligibility cases, benchmark stability and actual source samples; test cards, details, search, filters, map, comparisons, export and mobile. Apply any Idaho score-column changes only under `docs/idaho-scoring-rollout.md`, including a fresh read-only snapshot, exact stale-protected plan and non-score verification. Preserve all historical data and leave automatic sync disabled.

## B&C workbook found in chat history

The owner pointed to an earlier conversation with the [BC Elk workbook](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1Vi7R6feUpZwJ0Lat1xnvZYOsRMcWnFAzTtV6Mw_KuBk/edit). The read-only workbook has `ELK`, `Pronghorn`, and `Mule Deer` tabs. The guide cites samples of 1,957, 4,403 and 3,743, so these counts cannot be reproduced without its filters, extraction date and treatment of dates such as January 1. Records include US and Canadian locations, and the guide's geographic inclusion is not specified. The workbook was not modified. Record-book entries are selected records rather than a denominator of hunts or hunting effort.

`trophyV2Shadow.ts` contains an isolated, non-production experiment for the documented pillar arithmetic and small-cohort shrinkage. It returns unknown when the inputs do not establish a timing score, county evidence or, for a small cohort, a comparable all-weapon percentile. There is no private-land deduction or weapon offset. A missing harvest proxy uses the guide's explicit neutral value and is flagged. The function is not wired to displayed or stored scores; no state adapters, peer cohorts, user-facing ratings or production score writes are implied by its presence.

### Utah shadow audit

`node --import tsx scripts/audit-ut-trophy-v2.ts out/ut-v2-shadow.json` evaluates the checked-in Utah 2026 hunt snapshot for resident and nonresident pools. It requires one unit, one certain season segment, an agreeing supplied/geographic crosswalk, one active reference and permits in the pool. Where the reference has different state and overall county tiers, it runs both as separate scenarios and reports a single score only if the results are identical; it does not select a preferred county basis. It assumes no independently verified source or agency-age signal and uses the guide's neutral harvest value. These are shadow estimates, not validated ratings or live data.

### November 16 corrections

Four parts of the first shadow audit were wrong and have been changed:

- **Timing used the season's midpoint.** A Sep 1–Nov 30 rifle deer season, open through the whole rut, scored 7 while a 3-day Nov 20–22 hunt scored 15; an Aug 15–Dec 31 archery deer season was unknown. `utV2ShadowTiming` now scores the best rubric window the segment actually overlaps, day by day. Days the rubric doesn't list add nothing; a segment with no listed day stays unknown. 108 of the 446 previously scored rows changed timing (mostly 13 or 14 → 15, and pronghorn archery 10 → 13.5), and 63 rows the midpoint left unknown are now scored. Not yet modelled: how much of a season falls in the window, and separate segments.
- **Demand used raw application counts,** which ranks a 2,000-permit hunt above a 5-permit hunt with 2,000 applicants: it measured size and ease of drawing. `trophyV2DemandRatio.ts` ranks **applicants per permit** in the same species, year, category, pool and weapon (all weapons in the category below ten peers, still ten or more). Example: Wasatch Mtns West general buck deer had 0.94 applicants per permit (more permits than applicants) and scored 82.7 Excellent on raw counts; it now scores 59. The trade-off: a large-quota premium unit spreads its demand, so Manti limited-entry elk falls from 84.7 to 58.4. Applicants per permit still reflects price, points and access as well as animal quality; it is a demand signal, not trophy size. Points needed to draw is the alternative to test next.
- **The limited-entry floor was circular.** It floored Utah limited-entry hunts at 76/68 using the supplied CSV's own Elite/Excellent tier, the answer the model is meant to check; 139 of 446 scored rows sat exactly on it. Removed (`utV2ShadowFloor` is gone).
- **Confidence:** unverified source tags no longer lift a hunt to Moderate on their own; Moderate now needs a verified hunt boundary as well. (The audit already passed zero independent sources, so no row was Verified; the earlier claim that tag counts produced "Verified" was wrong.)

Tried and rejected: leaving the unknown harvest pillar out and rescaling the other 90 points to 100. It lowers the effective Elite bar from 80 to 76.5 raw points and moved 48 rows into Elite on that alone. A constant five can't separate hunts, but it only sets where the cut-offs fall; the cut-offs themselves are uncalibrated and remain open.

Result on the same snapshot: 1,234 hunt/pool rows; 509 scored across 262 hunts (78 pronghorn, 13 on the all-weapon cohort), up from 446. Tiers: Elite 39, Excellent 212, Good 161, Standard 77, and 20 county-basis rows whose two scenarios differ in tier (was Elite 29, Excellent 248, Good 109, Standard 50, 10 differing); 193 of the 446 previously scored rows changed tier. Unscored: 376 no supplied crosswalk match, 90 incomplete geometry, 78 ambiguous names, 72 multiple or uncertain season segments, 32 not antlered, 20 under ten comparable hunts, 18 multi-unit, 15 no permits in the pool, 14 county tier missing, 6 timing not covered, 4 crosswalk conflicts. No production scores were calculated or written.

The land-access warning uses mapped public plus enrolled access where a compatible estimate exists. When only public ownership is known, it says private access is not fully mapped; ownership is not a guarantee of legal or physical access. It is a warning, not a new score adjustment. The existing Quality model's separately documented access component is unchanged.
