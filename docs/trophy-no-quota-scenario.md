# Trophy scoring: no-quota scenario

This is an offline experiment, not the production model. The owner clarified that fewer tags must not improve trophy score, timing should have little weight, private land must not reduce trophy scores, and ordinary timing must be the same across weapons. Early any-weapon deer dates are only a candidate velvet exception, not verified velvet condition.

Production at `aa6998f` uses Idaho applicants per tag (35 points) and rut timing (15 points); production Idaho table and stored scores therefore still require a coordinated correction. The scripts below do not update them. Replacing the table without the fresh protected score-only database plan would leave the site inconsistent.

## Reproducible scenarios

- `node --import tsx scripts/audit-id-trophy-no-quota.ts out/id-no-quota-shadow.json`: exactly the 236 hunts in the existing Idaho v2 table, using the retained November 16 snapshot. Demand is held constant; the herd weight is 47, publications 8, B&C 5 and timing at most 5. These weights are a test, not calibrated. It intentionally refuses to write a production table. Idaho source data supplies outer season dates, so split-season gaps are not resolved.
- `node --import tsx scripts/audit-ut-trophy-no-quota.ts out/ut-no-quota-shadow.json`: Utah retained snapshot and crosswalk; demand is constant, timing has at most a two-point spread, and actual reported season segments are inspected separately. It does not deduce a season across gaps. Results duplicate resident and nonresident scenarios for the same hunt; a scenario does not establish pool availability.

## Results and remaining work

On the pinned Idaho table population, tier ranking now uses the score before timing; this prevents a small timing change from putting hunts with identical unit evidence into different tiers. The total numeric shadow score still incorporates up to five timing points. This choice creates ties and needs review: it can move several hunts across a tier boundary together. The mature-animal unit proxy uses the maximum observed hunt share and can favour units with more hunts. The current weights, rank thresholds and missing-animal assumptions require review before use. The fixed-demand scenario does not prove the other inputs are unaffected by tag management, nor does it remove quota effects from production models in other states.

Utah produced 480 stable score rows and 138 county-basis scenario rows out of 1,234 hunt/pool rows; 380 lack a supplied crosswalk match, 90 have incomplete geometry and 46 ambiguous supplied matches. The match now respects word boundaries, fixing 16 Southwest Desert hunts that were also matching West Desert, and rejects an erroneous generic Plateau name match for Old Woman Plateau CWMU. Those two CWMU hunts still lack a supplied match; the geometry alone is not verified identity. Other rows have unverified timing, season, unit, county or eligibility inputs. Paunsaugunt DB1001, DB1004, DB1006 and DB1008 yield 75.5 Excellent in this provisional scenario against 100 Elite in the prior live classification. This reflects uncalibrated formula assumptions, including constant demand and missing harvest evidence; it is not evidence that the live rating should be lowered. The live source mapping and all unit-specific data must be verified before any such move.

Remaining: inspect differences and state sources, identify verified early velvet hunts, resolve crosswalk and season ambiguities, assess other states and retained pinned exceptions, design and test a consistent model and presentation, recalculate stored Idaho scores only through the protected workflow, deploy and verify. The owner's western v5 spreadsheet remains an audit reference and is not modified.

## Additional Idaho evidence check

The live public export was rechecked for the 237 retained snapshot hunts: all matched on the available harvest and hunter counts, antler-share figures, units, dates and draw year (subject to the export's rounding). The file's prior live trophy tiers are older than the current deployed table, so comparisons use the table, not its `liveTrophy` field.

A second offline trial uses only single-unit hunts for unit antler context and selects the largest available harvest sample, rather than taking the highest observed share or copying a multi-unit hunt into every unit. The available single-unit data does not support an animal-share measure for 22 of 108 deer hunts, 24 of 78 elk hunts, and all 50 pronghorn hunts in the Idaho table. The neutral values in the trial are not observations and must not be described as measured animal evidence.

Unit 54 shows why the choice is material: hunt 1042 reports 51.8% four-point-or-better out of 890 harvested deer, while hunt 1043 reports 85.9% out of 87. The larger-sample rule would change both the October and November hunts to Standard, while selecting the highest season share produces Elite for both. Neither result establishes the unit's inherent trophy ceiling; the two hunts differ in season and hunter selection. The single-unit trial therefore remains a sensitivity check, not a production table.

IDFG's [mule deer advisory committee notes](https://idfg.idaho.gov/sites/default/files/mule-deer-advisory-committee-meeting-notes_aug-5_2024.pdf) say season dates, season length and weapon influence the reported four-point share. IDFG's [mule deer management plan](https://idfg.idaho.gov/sites/default/files/plan-deer-mule-2020-25.pdf) identifies Units 54 and 55 as the South Hills population and discusses different early and rut hunts, while agency [composition-survey guidance](https://idfg.idaho.gov/press/how-does-my-hunt-unit-look-deer-and-elk-biologists-have-many-ways-know-answer) explains that buck counts can be conservative and are sampled on multi-unit population areas. These sources support treating the recorded shares as hunt-specific evidence rather than a direct unit genetics measurement; they do not provide a validated replacement score or certify a specific tier for either hunt.
