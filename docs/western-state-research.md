# Western state onboarding research

Research leads supplied by the owner, recorded 2026-11-07. These are starting points, not authorization to import data or enable sync. Regulations, URLs, species coverage, quotas, reporting methods and access terms can change. Before implementing a state, verify the current official rules and actual reports for each hunt, species, year and applicant pool; record provenance and validation evidence. Some links below are leads supplied by the owner and have not yet been checked for current availability or coverage.

The long-term goal is a unified search for any supported species across any selection of integrated states, including all integrated states together. Use the existing Idaho and Colorado integrations, shared finder and database as the starting point, extending reusable patterns to other states while accounting for differences in coverage and draw systems. This is a direction for future work, not a request to import every state now.

Search by actual species and hunt type, not assumed state-wide availability. Determine whether data is an agency estimate, survey count, applicant count, license count or hunter-season count; retain source, vintage, applicable units and caveats.

For each state, maintain an onboarding inventory of available years, species, hunt and unit identifiers, draw pools, report formats, update cadence and missing data. Save representative source fixtures and expected results for parser and calculation tests. Track the source publication date separately from the season year and last successful import, and make stale or partial coverage visible. Preserve original source evidence and historical records when a report is revised.

Check boundary changes, merged or split units, multi-unit hunts and season dates before joining reports across years or mapping harvest to a hunt. Do not assign an entire multi-unit harvest or hunter count to each individual unit as a local observation; label broader-area estimates and avoid duplicate aggregation. Verify access permissions, data license and attribution for each source.

## Draw modeling cautions

- Do not implement the supplied `1 - (1 - tickets / total tickets) ^ quota` as a universal exact odds formula. It assumes independent draws with a fixed ticket share; real draws can remove winning applicants and their entries, use different pools, process multiple choices or apply quotas and party rules. A simulation must reproduce the relevant official algorithm, disclose assumptions and uncertainty, and be checked against published results. Use unknown or a clearly qualified estimate when available reports do not support an exact calculation.
- Preference queues and bonus-point systems vary by hunt and allocation phase. A simple cutoff calculation applies only to a verified strict preference phase with compatible counts; it does not cover a separate random phase, party applications or quota restrictions.
- Montana FWP says participating bonus points are squared plus one application chance; it also describes a separate nonresident combination preference system with a distinct random allocation. Verify participation and current-year details in [FWP's points guidance](https://fwp.mt.gov/buyandapply/hunting-licenses/bonus-points) and [FAQ](https://fwp.mt.gov/buyandapply/licenseandpermitfaq).
- Nevada's [2026 application FAQ](https://www.ndow.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026-Big-Game-Application-FAQ.pdf) describes squared bonus points plus one draw number, with party-specific handling. Recheck the applicable year's rules.
- Oregon's [2026 regulations](https://myodfw.com/sites/default/files/2025-11/26_BG_regs.pdf) describe 75% preference and 25% random first-choice phases for specified hunt series, with exceptions; do not generalize to every species or hunt.
- New Mexico's [draw explanation](https://wildlife.dgf.nm.gov/hunting/applications-and-draw-information/how-new-mexico-draw-works/) describes resident, outfitter and nonresident pools and sequential processing of each application's choices. A quota divided by applicants is at most a rough indicator, not an exact general-purpose odds model.
- The supplied Utah, Arizona, Washington and Wyoming allocation and point descriptions are unverified leads, not implementation specifications. Confirm each applicable species, pool, choice, year and phase from current agency rules and reports.

## Harvest, access and environmental research

- The owner identified Montana survey nonresponse as a concern. Examine agency methodology and use the appropriate finalized agency estimates and uncertainty where available; do not automatically apply independent reweighting or imputation to already adjusted estimates. Any new statistical adjustment needs justified source data, methods and validation.
- Montana harvest data has been flagged by the owner as unreliable and reported every other year. Treat this as an onboarding concern to verify against current FWP methodology and each report's actual years, species and geography; do not assume missing years represent zero or duplicate biennial observations into annual results. Investigate and validate separate or supplemental metrics and scoring approaches appropriate to Montana, disclose data limitations, and leave harvest-derived values or scores unavailable when the evidence is inadequate. Do not silently substitute an unrelated metric or change the shared Quality model without documenting and testing the state-specific treatment.
- Harvest success should use compatible harvested-animal and hunter-afield measures when available, not silently substitute license holders for hunters. Preserve agency definitions and note reporting limitations.
- Candidate spatial sources: [BLM geospatial data](https://www.blm.gov/services/geospatial/GISData), [BLM GIS services](https://gis.blm.gov/natl/rest/services/), [USFS geodata](https://data.fs.usda.gov/geodata/) (including Road Core and motor vehicle use maps), [Census TIGER/Line](https://www.census.gov/geographies/mapping-files/time-series/geo/tiger-line-file.html), and [USGS National Map](https://apps.nationalmap.gov/downloader/). Verify coverage, update dates, licensing and jurisdiction before use. Public ownership, physical road presence and legal motorized or public access are different facts; seasonal closures and local restrictions need authoritative evidence.
- Public-land density and whole-unit density have different denominators. The existing DrawOdds density must not be silently changed: introduce and label a public-land-based estimate separately only if its hunter and acreage assumptions are supportable. Whole-unit hunter totals cannot establish where hunters actually hunted.
- Optional future environmental research: [SNODAS snow data](https://nsidc.org/data/g02158), [PRISM climate data](https://prism.oregonstate.edu/), [NWS GIS](https://www.weather.gov/gis/) and agency winter-range polygons. Spatial intersections and seasonal indices can describe conditions but do not by themselves validate predictions of herd health, winter mortality or future quotas. Check resolution, access terms, coverage and predictive evidence before presenting such claims.
