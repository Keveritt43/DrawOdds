# Western trophy-source input

`data/trophy-source-western-v3.csv` preserves the owner's supplied source list, including states that DrawOdds does not yet support. Its SHA-256 is `2c0e19fcce0f5a479e5a46d5ffeb4ff8153c065d4d8ae23c0339faf58b3d41af`. The 2,422 rows cover AZ, CA, CO, ID, MT, NM, NV, OR, UT, WA and WY; inclusion does not mean that any state is onboarded.

The file is an evidence lead, not a validated unit classification. Provider tags and B&C tiers are supplied claims without individual source links, definitions for the tier thresholds, or retrieval dates. County associations do not locate a record within any unit. Source tags are not necessarily independent.

The November 14 replacement attachment has exactly the same parsed rows as the prior file. Its 79 Utah records are all tagged `BC`; they identify management-unit numbers, while the application's Utah hunt boundaries have separate IDs. Do not equate those IDs. Utah DWR's [deer management plans](https://wildlife.utah.gov/deer/plans) identify the named management units, while the [hunt planner](https://wildlife.utah.gov/biggame) provides current hunt-specific boundaries.

`data/trophy-ut-wmu-crosswalk.json` records 288 candidate species-scoped links derived from DWR's species-specific management polygons and the retained hunt-boundary geometries. A link requires at least 90% of the hunt boundary to fall inside one management polygon with a single numeric parent key. Combined management keys, split boundaries, missing source units and unmatched geometries remain unknown. For example, deer boundary 607 is almost entirely management unit 1, but boundary 1 is Antelope Island, not management unit 1. The crosswalk is geographic context, not the location of individual B&C records, and needs hunt-level review before use in scoring. Regenerate the overlap candidates using `scripts/review-ut-trophy-crosswalk.cjs` and convert them with `scripts/prepare-ut-trophy-crosswalk.py`; both are read-only with respect to production.

Keep unknown or unsupported demand distinct from observed low demand. Historical first-choice counts, all-choice counts, and incompatible residency pools must not be mixed; a source-list entry or lack of an entry is not proof of animal size. Do not wire this file directly into production scoring without a reviewed classification, season-specific evidence, consistency checks across the finder and details, and the applicable production scoring safeguards.

`data/trophy-demand-review-2026.json` is a review artifact, not a current production snapshot. `scripts/prepare-exceptional-trophy-demand.py` records source digests and derives same-state/species/year/weapon-or-category cohorts from archived Idaho and Colorado snapshots and the checked-in MT, UT, WA and WY reports. It requires at least 20 distinct comparable peers. The exceptional hunt screen is the nearest-rank 95th percentile and can include multi-unit hunts; the unit screen uses the 75th percentile of each unit's maximum direct single-unit hunt count. A multi-unit hunt's applications cannot be attributed to each individual unit. Washington uses separately identified all-choice applications; the other included states use first-choice counts. WA requires stable same-code and same-name/category/area/tag identity across 2025–2026 and matching printed counts.

The earlier workbook excluded Wyoming because its stored resident and nonresident values are reported in different phases. Further review of the [WGFD application process](https://wgfd.wyo.gov/licenses-applications/application-process), the [Wyoming draw data model](states/wy-draw-model.md), and the retained reports supports a narrower valid combination: resident first-choice applicants plus nonresident regular and special preference-phase first-choice applicants. For every included pool the script verifies that preference applicants minus preference awards equal random first-choice applicants. The random phase is a subset of those nonresident applicants and is **not** added again. Hunts missing a pool or failing reconciliation stay unknown, as do landowner and omitted report categories.

These screens describe demand, not statistical significance or trophy size. Archived counts must be compared with a fresh read-only production snapshot before any score write.

`trophyCandidateTier`, `trophyCandidateResolver` and `trophyCandidateSeason` implement the proposed Scenario A and the Colorado season illustration in isolation. They are deliberately not wired into finder, map, score, detail or export paths. Their output is provisional for missing inputs and unverified source claims. A completed rollout must reconcile legacy explicit hunt exceptions, review impacts across all supported species, perform end-to-end consistency and mobile/desktop UAT, and coordinate Idaho's stored score updates using `docs/idaho-scoring-rollout.md`. In particular, production database credentials are not exposed by the current Railway OAuth connector, which returns variable names only; a safe authenticated read-only snapshot path is still needed before a production scoring plan can be generated.
