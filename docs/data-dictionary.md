# DrawOdds data dictionary

**Washington mixed deer permits:** WDFW's 2026 "any buck, white-tailed antlerless" special permits allow both antlered deer and antlerless whitetail, not antlerless-only or whitetail-only. The finder corrects the retained classification at read time for that exact 2026 legal wording; both antler filters include it. Species-specific trophy is withheld because the buck may be either deer type. The agency legal wording remains visible. Future imports correct the same retained source, and the builder classifies it correctly. [2026 WDFW regulations](https://wdfw.wa.gov/sites/default/files/publications/02706/wdfw02706.pdf), p. 35.

This describes the current Idaho and Colorado implementation, not a universal definition of every agency's data. Check the cited code and state-specific sources when changing a metric; update this document and the user-facing explanation alongside any calculation change. Values may be unknown, estimated, pooled or based on broader geography than an individual hunt. Show the applicable source, years, population and limitations rather than silently filling gaps.

## Draw odds

- **Meaning and units:** Estimated or observed chance of drawing a specified license, expressed to users as a percentage; calculations use a fraction from 0 to 1. The applicable applicant pool and method determine the meaning.
- **Inputs:** State, species, hunt, residency, eligibility, draw year and choice; points, youth status and draw phase or pool when applicable. Published applicants and successful draws, quotas or point-pool results are evidence—not automatically interchangeable quantities.
- **Idaho:** Combined search uses historical pooled controlled-draw odds and the available general-tag odds appropriate to residency. Capped resident availability can be unknown; it is not automatically guaranteed.
- **Colorado:** Uses the applicable residency, species points and pool with the selected historical or projected model. Point-level results and projections are distinct; a cutoff, general/OTC eligibility or past success does not guarantee a future tag.
- **Limitations:** Results depend on source and reporting years, choices, pool definitions and model assumptions; past outcomes and projections are not promises. An absent pool or unsupported calculation is unknown, not 0% or 100%. See `src/lib/multiStateSearch.ts`, state odds adapters and `docs/multi-state-search.md`.

## Harvest success

**Wyoming deer:** The existing import combines mule and white-tailed deer harvest and uses the larger species-specific hunter count as a proxy for their unknown overlap. That ratio is an upper bound, not a verified combined success rate. Until a compatible denominator is established, Wyoming deer success, hunter density and dependent scores are suppressed at read time; the stored observations are preserved. The separate WGFD tables remain the source: https://wgfd.wyo.gov/media/33467/download.

- **Meaning and units:** Harvested animals divided by hunters afield in compatible reported data; stored as a fraction and displayed as a percentage. `success5` generally reflects pooled available counts over up to five seasons: total harvest ÷ total hunters, not an unweighted average of annual percentages.
- **Inputs:** Agency hunter and harvest estimates or counts, species, geography, season and available years. `hunters5`, `harvest5` and `harvestYears` describe the underlying window where available.
- **Idaho:** Hunt or area summaries use the applicable IDFG data and historical boundary handling; inspect the summary and harvest implementations for the specific hunt.
- **Colorado:** CPW estimates can be unit/season aggregates rather than exact hunt-code results; overlapping or multi-unit data must retain their reported scope and limitations. Some years may be unavailable or excluded for ambiguity.
- **Limitations:** Survey estimates and sample sizes vary. Tag holders are not automatically hunters afield; pooled windows and different source geographies or years may not be comparable. Missing or invalid evidence is unknown. See `src/sync/steps/summary.ts`, `src/sync/co/harvest.ts` and `docs/colorado-rollout.md`.

## Hunter density and elbow room

- **Meaning and units:** Estimated hunters per square mile per year. Where pooled counts and area are available, density is `(hunters5 / harvestYears) / (acres / 640)`. This is generally an area-wide average, not a count of people encountered or an estimate confined to public land.
- **Inputs:** Compatible hunter counts, number of reported years and acreage. The Quality model transforms density into the 0–100 **Elbow room** component; lower density yields a higher component score.
- **Idaho:** Uses available hunt/area or unit summary counts and acreage; multi-unit or zone metrics may describe broader geography.
- **Colorado:** Uses reported unit/season counts and area estimates. For deer with confirmed overlapping general/OTC elk seasons, the read-time pressure calculation can include elk unit-season participation. CPW's all-license elk count is a proxy, not an exact OTC count or unique-person count; missing or ambiguous evidence must remain visible.
- **Limitations:** Hunter-seasons may count a person more than once, and broad-area counts cannot establish where people hunted. A public-land-only density would be a different, separately labeled metric. See `src/lib/coLand.ts`, `src/lib/coPressure.ts` and `docs/colorado-elk-pressure.md`.

## Public land and access

- **Meaning and units:** `publicPct` is the estimated share of the relevant area classified as public land, on a 0–100 percentage scale. It is not a guarantee of legal access, road access or huntability.
- **Inputs:** Land-ownership classification, geometry and acreage for the unit, hunt area or covered units; data sources, coverage and attribution vary by state. Multi-unit aggregation can be an estimate rather than an exact hunt boundary.
- **Idaho:** `huntablePct` can include public land plus the applicable Access Yes! share, capped at 100%; it is distinct from public land alone. Quality uses `huntablePct` when available, otherwise `publicPct`.
- **Montana:** `huntablePct` = public land + Block Management **Type 1** (self sign-in) on land that is not already public, for BMAs FWP's guide lists for the hunt's species; `accessYesAcres/Count/Pct` hold the Type 1 figures. **Type 2** (landowner/FWP permission) is never included; it is read at render time from `data/mt/bma.json`. See `docs/states/mt-onboarding.md` §16.
- **Quick Start accessible-land minimum:** `minAccessLand` uses Idaho's public-plus-Access Yes area estimate and Montana's public acreage plus species-specific Type 1 Block Management (2026–27). It uses public acreage alone in other states, whose private access programs are not yet included. Unknown required acreage does not pass a minimum. Idaho's existing Access Yes estimate is a geographic proxy; public ownership and enrollment do not establish legal or physical access for every hunt. Check property rules. This is separate from the public-only `minPublic` filter.
- **Colorado:** Uses validated state/unit ownership information; the adapter checks coverage and shares and gives private-only hunts zero public-access credit for scoring. Colorado does not inherit Idaho's Access Yes! program.
- **Limitations:** Ownership classification and access rights are different, and surface access, private inholdings, seasonal restrictions and reporting coverage matter. See `src/lib/coLand.ts`, `src/lib/coScores.ts` and `src/sync/steps/summary.ts`.

## Wilderness share (Wyoming)

- **Meaning and units:** `pct` in `data/wy/wilderness.json` is the share of a Wyoming hunt area that lies inside federally designated wilderness, 0–100. It drives the nonresident guide note on Wyoming unit and hunt pages: W.S. 23-2-401(a) bars a nonresident from hunting big or trophy game inside designated wilderness without a licensed professional guide or a resident guide (one guide per two nonresidents). The rule follows the wilderness boundary; the share only says how much of the area it covers.
- **Inputs:** Wilderness Connect (wilderness.net) boundaries for the 15 wildernesses touching Wyoming, all Forest Service, and the hunt-area map boundaries in `data/wy/units.json`, both in EPSG:5070. Built by `scripts/wy_build_wilderness.mjs`; checks: the 13 Wyoming-only wildernesses integrate within 1.5% of their published acreage (actual: within 0.3%), and hunt-area acreage within the `wy_build_areas.py` tolerances.
- **Not counted:** overlaps under 1,000 acres per wilderness, which are slivers where a generalized hunt-area boundary follows a wilderness edge (largest 526 ac; smallest real overlap 3,961 ac). They are listed under `slivers`. Wilderness study areas, roadless areas and national parks are not designated wilderness and are not included.
- **Not a score input.** Read at render time; production rows are not changed. Idaho's `wildernessPct` (IDFG unit pages) is a different source and Idaho has no such guide rule.

## Quality

**Trophy tiers:** eligible deer, elk and pronghorn use Elite (100), Excellent (80), Good (65), or Standard (50). The candidate base considers three signals: at least two named non-B&C publications, both supplied 10-year county tiers at 1 as one county-context signal, and high comparable hunt demand. All three can produce Elite; alternatively, two named sources and a matched hunt application count in the top decile of its comparable state, species, year and category can produce provisional Elite without the county tiers. Two signals support Excellent; one or a named source supports Good. Top-decile demand describes hunter interest, not a measurement of animal size; comparisons have no minimum group size; those with fewer than 30 peers are provisional and can be unstable, especially when a hunt is the only observation. Publications are not presumed independent. An input Tier 2 can also support Good. B&C-only with measured low demand is Standard; missing demand defaults to provisional Standard. An unlisted hunt with exceptional same-category demand may receive Good at the hunt level. The supplied source claims and county tiers have not been independently verified; B&C county associations do not locate individual records, and demand does not measure animal size. Antlerless and maximum-antler-restricted hunts are excluded, including Wyoming restrictions written as “three (3) points or less.” Eligible Wyoming Type 1 and other unrestricted antlered or any-sex licenses are evaluated using their own hunt evidence; a type number alone does not establish trophy potential. A multi-unit hunt with differing unit tiers uses a conservative floor marked provisional; map shading classifies each unit separately, and applications belong to the whole hunt. Standard is not evidence of low trophy potential; empty unit identity is unknown. The bonus is `(tier − 50) / 10 × harvest / (harvest + 50)`, bounded above by 5 and subject to the existing Quality ceiling. Idaho stored scores require a coordinated scores-only rollout. Sources, cohort construction, coverage and Utah crosswalk limitations are described in `docs/trophy-source-western-v3.md`.

**Idaho 2026 controlled-hunt table:** The current table supersedes the legacy Idaho fallback below for matching hunts. It ranks hunts partly using the highest sample-adjusted mature-antler harvest share from any associated hunt, which can cover several units or a different season. That selection does not measure unit genetics or establish the herd's age distribution. The existing tiers are displayed as provisional; the numerical model and stored scores have not been changed by this labeling.

**Idaho deer seasonal rule:** an eligible controlled hunt open during November receives Trophy 80 when its estimated five-year share of harvested antlered mule deer with at least four points exceeds 70% and both its resident and nonresident five-year first-choice draw odds are below 5%. The verified 2026 Idaho early controlled hunt 1044 in Unit 55 receives Trophy 80 as a reviewed exception. Other reviewed November exceptions do not upgrade a B&C-only association with low or unknown demand. Separate season windows are evaluated independently under the seasonal adjustment below. Missing or invalid dates, antler shares, or odds do not qualify for the November upgrade. The share is derived from rounded agency harvest estimates and requires at least 20 antlered animals in its denominator. This rule ranks trophy tiers, not overall Quality, which also incorporates harvest success, access and crowding.

**Idaho 2026 hunt 1044 area correction:** IDFG identifies this early deer hunt as Unit 55 only. A stale stored summary also associates Unit 57; the read path excludes that association without rewriting historical geography. Hunt-area access, land and density derived from the two-unit geometry remain unknown, so Quality and Opportunity are unavailable until those inputs are rebuilt. The independently reviewed Excellent trophy tier and historical draw and harvest data remain visible.

**Season and draw evidence:** Outside Arizona and Colorado, when actual dates are known, deer hunts outside November 1–14 or elk hunts outside September through October 7 drop one tier. Pronghorn is not subject to that seasonal adjustment. The nine 2026 Utah DWR premium limited-entry buck deer permits (DB1000–DB1008, including multiseason permits) are Elite in their designated hunt boundaries and seasons; management permits are not included. This classification is based on the agency hunt designation and does not guarantee animal size. Early Idaho Unit 55 is an exception. See the [Utah 2026 application guide](https://wildlife.utah.gov/pdf/guidebooks/biggameapp.pdf). Colorado compares the same species, weapon and numbered season where a matching historical application observation exists; below-top-quartile demand lowers the candidate tier by one level, while source-scope or restricted-hunt mismatches are provisional. Colorado and Wyoming point cutoffs do not change trophy tiers. Colorado scores are calculated at read time.

**Colorado premier elk regular seasons:** Units 2, 10 and 201 can receive provisional Elite for a regular-season elk hunt only when its exact 2026 code, unit and application total match a top-quartile observation among at least 30 comparable weapon/season peers. [GOHUNT's 2026 unit forecast](https://www.gohunt.com/browse/application-strategies/application-strategy-colorado-elk-and-antelope-draw-strategies-plus-tactics-to-help-you-apply) and [Huntin' Fool's overview](https://www.huntinfool.com/colorado-elk-hunting) support the area review; neither demand nor a unit forecast measures current hunt-level antler size. See [the preset review](colorado-elk-preset-review.md).

**Wyoming deer Peak Rut preset:** Deer hunts rated Good or better qualify if an actual reported archery or regular season segment overlaps November 1–14 and that same segment matches the selected method and date filters. An outer date envelope does not establish a season during a gap. Unknown or invalid segment dates do not qualify. This fixed seasonal window is a heuristic; rut timing varies by location and year. [Wyoming Game and Fish deer regulations](https://wgfd.wyo.gov/Regulations/Big-Game-Regulations/Deer-Seasons) provide the underlying season dates.

**Wyoming pronghorn Peak Rut preset:** The actual archery or regular segment must overlap September 11–30 and satisfy selected weapon and date filters in that same segment. The season envelope cannot make an October rifle season appear open in September. This window is an approximation, and breeding varies; [Wyoming Game and Fish](https://wgfd.wyo.gov/wyoming-wildlife/wyoming-wildlife-magazine/american-pronghorn-species-profile) describes September as the usual breeding season. Missing or invalid segments are unknown.

**Montana Tag Filler permit measure:** In qualifying mule deer permit districts, estimated mule bucks harvested divided by permits issued across the reported seasons can satisfy the preset's 60% threshold, alongside at least 65% public land. This is a permit-based estimate, not hunter success: not every permit holder participates, and agency harvest estimates have uncertainty. It is shown separately on cards and in exports and does not populate hunter success, Quality, Opportunity or the shared minimum-success filter. District and species proxies remain excluded from this permit rule.

Unreviewed early hunts retain their existing provisional fallback; a changed code, eligibility, area, year or dates does not inherit this exception.

**Descriptive draw and harvest screen:** For legacy Idaho cases outside the 2026 controlled-hunt table, where an eligible same-state, species, year, nonresident pool and draw-system group has a recorded comparison group, an observed overall draw rate at or above that group's 75th percentile is considered relatively high. Groups and years appear in `data/trophy-draw-benchmarks.json`; they are descriptive, not a significance test or a personal draw probability. When such a hunt also has at least three years and 50 pooled hunters, and observed harvest success is below 50%, the trophy tier is capped at Good; if an approximate 95% Wilson upper bound is also below 50%, the tier is Standard. The bound is a screening aid, not proof of significance: harvest estimates and repeated hunter-seasons may violate binomial assumptions. Missing or incompatible data are unknown and do not trigger the screen. No non-Idaho state uses this draw-rate screen. No zero is imputed.

**Washington Blue Mountains elk:** The early-season recognition uses GMUs 157, 162, 169 and 172 and the separately mapped East and West Wenaha hunt areas. Low draw odds or a small quota do not independently promote a hunt. A small sample or a multi-unit hunt cannot establish which unit produced an animal.

**Washington quality elk demand:** An early-season, unrestricted adult quality elk permit can also receive Excellent when its 2025 pamphlet application count is at or above the 95th percentile of that category (1,242 applications among 93 eligible hunts) and its overall award-to-application ratio is positive and below 2%. This is a descriptive demand screen, not a formal significance test or evidence of antler size; the ratio is not an individual's squared-point draw odds. The threshold is year-specific and unknown years do not qualify automatically. WDFW's April 22, 2026 corrections establish September 25–29 dates for the nine affected hunts; the application fixes their malformed extracted dates at read time. Colockum choice 2016 was an October–November hunt before 2023, while the current September season used choice 2015 in 2021–2022. The audited annual identity match links those September records and leaves the distinct late-season history archived. - **Meaning and units:** A 0–100 decision index for hunt quality, not a probability of harvest. Idaho uses species-specific absolute success benchmarks and components for success, access and elbow room.
- **Inputs and calculation:** The base is 65% Success, 20% Access and 15% Elbow room. A bounded, sample-adjusted trophy modifier applies only when suitable data exists; success-based ceilings prevent weak observed success from becoming elite through other factors. The model validates counts and minimum samples, and requires usable access and density for a Quality score.
- **Idaho:** Uses the shared model with applicable huntable/public access and eligible trophy evidence.
- **Colorado:** Applies validation and land/pressure handling before the shared model; unsupported trophy data receives no trophy adjustment. Confirmed general/OTC elk overlap can apply an additional Quality reduction to deer hunts, with the pressure context and limitations explained. Scoring is computed on copies at read time in the Colorado adapter.
- **Limitations:** Scores depend on source quality, geography, available years and species calibration; they are not guaranteed predictions. Missing required inputs leave Quality unknown. See `src/lib/score.ts`, `src/lib/coScores.ts` and `docs/colorado-elk-pressure.md`.

## Opportunity

- **Meaning and units:** A 0–100 decision index combining Quality with the applicable hunter's tag odds: `round(Quality × sqrt(odds))`, where odds is a fraction between 0 and 1. It cannot exceed Quality and is not a draw or harvest probability.
- **Inputs:** Valid Quality and applicable personalized odds, including residency and points/pool where relevant. If either is unknown, Opportunity is unknown.
- **State differences:** Idaho uses the applicable Idaho odds basis; Colorado uses the selected point-level historical or projected odds and applicant pool. Do not treat distinct odds bases as identical forecasts.
- **Limitations:** It inherits the uncertainty and coverage of Quality and odds. Personalization must not change cached public facts or another user's data. See `src/lib/score.ts`, `src/lib/multiStateSearch.ts` and `docs/multi-state-search.md`.

## Related metric: draw and fill

Where supported, **draw and fill** is estimated as applicable draw odds × harvest success, displayed as a percentage. It is distinct from Opportunity and inherits both inputs' assumptions, years and geographic limitations. Use compatible populations and periods, explain it as an estimate, and never promise a harvested animal.

Wyoming deer hunt-type reports give each deer species its own table (harvest ÷ active licenses, which WGFD heads "active lics/htrs"). A license holder who hunted both species is counted in each, so the species rates are never added. Where one species is at least 60% of the reported harvest (Idaho's 40%/60% harvest-mix thresholds), that species' five-season rate is the hunt's hunter success, density and score input (`withoutUnvalidatedWyDeerMetrics`, Nov 18, 2026). Mixed hunts, region aggregates and hunts without species records show the species rates only, with no combined success, density or Quality. The harvest-mix filter is not an eligibility filter. A license with no WGFD table for one species (Type 3 whitetail-only licenses have no mule deer table) counts as taking none of that species; before Nov 18, 2026 such licenses had no mix and no success at all.

**Wyoming elk boundary changes:** WGFD combined area 53 with 54 and moved part of 54 into 65 for 2024; for 2025 it changed areas 117, 123 and 126. License-level harvest observations for hunts covering these areas use only post-change years (2024 onward for 54/65, 2025 onward for 117/123/126). Region licenses that include these areas use the latest applicable start year. The original observations remain stored and are not displayed as if they described the current boundaries. Source: [WGFD 2024 hunt forecast](https://wgfd.wyo.gov/news-events/hunters-2024-wyoming-hunt-forecast-available), [2025 Sheridan regulations](https://wgfd.wyo.gov/news-events/hunting-regulation-changes-sheridan-region), and [Black Hills elk management report](https://wgfd.wyo.gov/media/32990/download?inline=). Some resulting rates describe only one or two seasons and must not be labeled five-year rates.

**Wyoming elk and antelope license-type reports:** WGFD's by-area tables head the denominator "ACTIVE LICS/HTRS", and the Commission allows one full-price license per species from the initial drawing (Ch. 2 §4), so a license-type row's harvest ÷ active licenses is that license's hunter success, the same quantity Idaho shows per hunt ([2025 elk report](https://wgfd.wyo.gov/media/33482/download)). It drives success filters, density, draw-and-fill and Quality like every other state. Region sums (`harvestBasis`) add area-license rows and can repeat holders across areas, so they stay withheld unless WGFD's own region survey supplies them (`verifiedRegionHarvest`). #181/#184 (Nov 15) had withheld every row; that was reversed on Nov 18, 2026 after the owner reported Wyoming harvest wasn't working. Trophy tiers are calculated separately. The raw source observations are retained.

**Wyoming Tag Filler:** For license-level records, the preset uses pooled harvest divided by active licenses for up to five reported seasons, requiring at least 60%, alongside 65% public land. For deer, either separately reported species can meet the threshold; species denominators are never added. Region aggregates are excluded. The finder and CSV identify the basis and reporting period.

**Colorado incomplete unit coverage (2026-11 audit):** A read-only comparison of production hunt histories against CPW's stored 2019–2024 unit/season tables found 331 observations on 107 multi-unit hunts whose reported table omitted one or more licensed units. Those exact observations are excluded on read, with their original values retained; later or otherwise changed source records are not overwritten by a stale correction. Future builds require a unique row for every listed unit. The displayed success and dependent scores are recalculated from remaining compatible observations.

**Montana deer proxies:** FWP mule-buck harvest divided by permits issued and single-species harvest divided by all deer hunters are useful district context but do not have a matching count of hunters pursuing that hunt. They are labeled separately and excluded from hunter-success filters, draw-and-fill, Quality and Opportunity. FWP district hunter density remains a separate all-license pressure measure.

**Wyoming nonresident general elk regions:** The 2025 and 2026 WGFD regulations section 6 list identical eligible area sets, which exclude limited-quota areas inside the broad region polygons. WGFD's 2025 elk harvest report table 5 directly estimates active region general licenses and harvest: E 719/962, S 511/1083, W 1506/2772. Only one general elk license per person was allowed. These are 2025 region-level rates, not unit-level rates; region land, density and Quality remain unknown until measured over the corrected eligible areas. **Colorado sex-specific harvest:** Pre-2025 all-sex unit tables do not provide a sex-specific hunter denominator. ## Idaho mapped-route and elevation screening (pilot)

The Idaho deer/elk finder can screen on estimated public-managed land in a unit more than one straight-line mile from mapped roads and designated motorized trails, optionally also above 9,000 feet. It reports the area in square miles. Elevation and distance must be true for the **same** cells. This is unit context, not acreage inside an individual hunt area; a multi-unit hunt passes if any one listed unit meets the threshold. Private-land-only hunts do not pass. The Idaho-only filter does not affect other states. No score uses these measures.

The offline build intersects 2026-downloaded IDFG unit geometry and BLM Idaho surface-management polygons, with a roughly 100-m equal-area grid (EPSG:5070), USGS 3DEP elevation in metres (9,000 ft = 2,743.2 m), USGS National Map road layers, BLM Idaho ground-transportation records where the asset or allowed-mode fields identify a road or motorized route, and Forest Service MVUM roads and motorized trails. The route buffer extends beyond each unit boundary. All source layers are current snapshots at retrieval; their underlying vintage varies. The public mask includes BLM, USFS, Idaho state, state fish/game or parks, Bureau of Reclamation, Corps and federally managed land-utilization classes; it excludes private, tribal, unknown, military, DOE, park and refuge classes. Public management does not establish ownership, hunting permission or physical access. `scripts/id-terrain-sources.py` and `scripts/id-terrain-pilot.py` document the processing; `data/id-terrain.json` is the read-only result. There are no database writes or scheduled syncs.

Road and trail coverage is incomplete, especially where BLM route classification is unknown; mapped roads can be private, closed or impassable, and motorized designations vary by vehicle and season. These are neither measured hunting pressure nor certified route access. A missing route can overstate remoteness; a closed or private road can understate it. Grid sampling and boundary generalization introduce additional error. Missing unit, land coverage below 98%, or insufficient elevation coverage means unknown, not zero. For Unit 76, USGS NHD Bear Lake (permanent identifier 120026431) supplies the water excluded by the surface-management layer; that water is excluded from the public-land mask. A measured zero is distinct. Do not assign the area in the entire unit to a hunt's actual legal boundary. The filter, card, details, map and exports use this same unit context.
